Reputation means nothing: the 2026 midseason shake-up

Miami sets the standard, LSU and UCLA crash the national picture from below, and some of college football's biggest names are fighting to stay up.

[Author] October 29, 2026 [X] min read Results through Oct 24 · Power ratings Oct 26
[Lead photo]

The 2026 college football season has reached a critical point, and one thing is becoming increasingly clear: reputation means very little in a league where every position matters.

With 138 programs competing across six divisions, the stakes extend well beyond the national championship. Promotion offers struggling programs a chance to climb toward college football's elite. Relegation threatens to send even the most recognizable names tumbling down the pyramid.

And after eight weeks of action, the landscape looks considerably different from what anyone might have expected entering the season.

Premier League: Miami sets the standard

The Miami Hurricanes have established themselves as the team to beat.

At 7-0, Miami occupies first place in the Premier League and holds the nation's No. 1 power rating at 22.34. The Hurricanes have combined consistency with convincing performances, building a legitimate case as the early favorite for both the Premier League title and the national championship.

But Miami's position is far from secure.

Notre Dame and Ohio State sit second and third in the Premier League standings, respectively, with Alabama, Indiana, Georgia, and unbeaten Utah completing a formidable group of contenders.

With three undefeated teams remaining nationally, including Miami, Utah, and UCLA, the race is entering its most compelling stretch.

The Premier League's biggest advantage remains the postseason format. Its top eight teams earn national playoff byes, making every position near the top of the table especially valuable.

The Championship: LSU looks ready for the big stage

Perhaps the most fascinating story in the entire pyramid is unfolding one division below the Premier League.

LSU entered the inaugural season in the College Championship, but the Tigers have quickly demonstrated that their talent belongs among the nation's elite.

Despite a 6-2 record, LSU currently ranks second nationally in our power ratings, trailing only Miami. Its 21.67 rating places the Tigers ahead of every Premier League team except the Hurricanes.

LSU is the clear Championship leader and the strongest candidate for automatic promotion.

Arizona, at 6-1, occupies the second automatic promotion position, while Houston is projected to claim the third spot through the promotion playoffs.

Tennessee, Wake Forest, and Missouri are all capable of disrupting those projections.

The bigger question is whether LSU could challenge for a national championship before ever earning a place in the Premier League.

Under the current format, that possibility is very real.

The biggest surprise: UCLA's remarkable rise

No program better demonstrates the unpredictability of this new system than UCLA.

Beginning the season in College League Two, the fourth tier, the Bruins have stormed to a perfect 7-0 record and climbed to ninth nationally in the power rankings.

For a program seeded 72nd entering the inaugural season, the rise has been extraordinary.

UCLA now leads League Two and appears well-positioned for automatic promotion. But the league's rules create an intriguing situation.

Even if the Bruins finish unbeaten and win the national championship, they can only advance one division next season.

That means a potential national champion could be playing in League One in 2027 rather than the Premier League.

It is exactly the kind of storyline that makes promotion-and-relegation football so compelling.

North Dakota State currently holds League Two's second automatic promotion position, while UCF is projected to win the promotion playoff. UCF's dramatic double-overtime victory over BYU has added another dimension to that race.

League One: Northwestern and Kentucky make their case

The third division has developed into one of the most competitive promotion battles in the country.

Northwestern, ranked tenth nationally with a 6-1 record, leads League One and looks increasingly capable of competing with higher-division programs.

Nebraska occupies the second automatic promotion position, but Kentucky might be the division's most dangerous postseason opponent.

The Wildcats are 7-1, rank 15th nationally in the power ratings, and are currently projected to secure the final promotion spot through the playoffs.

Boise State, Florida, and Wisconsin remain dangerous challengers.

While Northwestern and Nebraska control the automatic promotion positions, the battle for that third opening could be one of the best postseason competitions in the entire pyramid.

Lower divisions: new powers are emerging

In the College National League, fifth-tier Oklahoma State has become one of the season's biggest success stories.

Seeded 112th entering 2026, the Cowboys have climbed to 23rd nationally in the power ratings. They currently lead the division, with Liberty in the second automatic promotion position and Louisiana favored in the promotion playoffs.

In the sixth tier, Oregon State has built a strong case for promotion with a 5-2 record and the nation's 26th-best power rating.

Georgia State currently holds the second promotion spot, while Nevada leads the playoff projections.

Unlike teams in the four divisions immediately below the Premier League, sixth-tier programs cannot qualify for the 24-team national championship.

For Oregon State, promotion is therefore about more than climbing the pyramid. It represents the first step toward eventually gaining access to the national championship race.

Relegation watch: college football's giants are in trouble

While several lower-division programs are enjoying remarkable seasons, the Premier League's established powers are discovering the harsh reality of relegation.

Michigan, Washington, and Vanderbilt currently occupy the bottom three positions in the top division.

If the season ended today, all three would be relegated to the Championship.

Michigan's struggles are particularly surprising. Despite its history and national reputation, the Wolverines rank just 58th nationally in our current power ratings.

Washington sits 69th, and Vanderbilt has fallen to 96th.

Penn State, currently 17th in the Premier League, is immediately above the relegation zone and cannot afford to become complacent.

In this system, there are no exemptions for tradition, television audiences, or historical success.

A poor season carries real consequences.

National championship: a 24-team tournament with no easy road

The expanded national championship creates another layer of intrigue.

Twelve teams qualify from the Premier League, while the Championship, League One, League Two, and National League each provide three additional qualifiers. The top eight Premier League finishers receive opening-round byes.

If the season ended today, some of the projected opening-round matchups would include:

  • No. 9 LSUvs.No. 24 Louisiana
  • No. 10 UCLAvs.No. 23 UCF
  • No. 11 Northwesternvs.No. 22 North Dakota State
  • No. 15 Kentuckyvs.No. 18 Texas Tech
  • No. 16 Ole Missvs.No. 17 BYU

These are projected matchups, not finalized postseason fixtures. Several participants would still have to win their respective promotion playoffs.

The format creates an enormous incentive to compete in the Premier League, where a top-eight finish provides a direct path to the Round of 16.

For teams like LSU and UCLA, however, the national championship offers an opportunity to prove that divisional placement does not necessarily reflect championship potential.

Analyst's verdict

As the season moves toward November, three programs stand out for very different reasons.

1

Miami remains the championship favorite. The Hurricanes have earned that position through an undefeated start and the strongest power rating in the country.

2

LSU is the most dangerous challenger outside the Premier League. The Tigers have the talent to compete nationally while pursuing a promotion that could fundamentally reshape next year's top division.

3

UCLA is the season's most compelling Cinderella story. An undefeated program competing in the fourth tier, with a possible national championship berth and a long climb still ahead, represents everything this league was designed to create.

But perhaps the biggest storyline isn't at the top of the rankings.

It's what happens at the bottom.

Because in this new college football landscape, winning a championship brings glory, but avoiding relegation can determine the future of an entire program.

The race for the national title is heating up. The battle for promotion is intensifying. And for some of college football's biggest names, the fight for survival has only just begun.
